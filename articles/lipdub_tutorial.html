THE VISION
What does LipDub do? OVERVIEW
LipDub generates realistic talking face videos driven by audio. Given a source face and an audio clip, it produces a video where the person speaks with accurate lip sync, natural expressions, and preserved identity. The key insight: split the problem into two stages — first predict facial landmarks from audio using a Diffusion Transformer with flow matching, then render photo-realistic frames conditioned on those landmarks.
Input → Output flow
"Why landmarks?" — direct vs. landmark-guided
🎯
Two stages, one goal

Stage 1 converts audio into a sequence of facial landmarks using a Diffusion Transformer trained with flow matching — landmarks are encoded/decoded through a learned Landmark VAE, and the DiT generates in that latent space. Stage 2 takes those landmarks plus reference images and renders photo-realistic video frames. By decoupling motion prediction from appearance rendering, each stage can focus on what it does best.

The core challenge: Talking face generation must simultaneously handle lip synchronization (matching visemes to phonemes), identity preservation (the face must look like the source person), and temporal coherence (no jitter between frames). LipDub addresses all three through its two-stage landmark-guided approach.
ARCHITECTURE
The Two-Stage Pipeline PIPELINE
LipDub decomposes talking face generation into landmark prediction (Stage 1) and video rendering (Stage 2). Stage 1 encodes landmarks into a latent space via a trained Landmark VAE, then uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with flow matching to generate landmark sequences conditioned on audio. Stage 2 uses an alignment-then-translation architecture with SPADE normalization to render photo-realistic frames.

AUDIO ENCODING
DCA-VAE Audio Encoder AUDIO
The audio encoder uses a DCA-VAE (Disentangled Contrastive Audio VAE) to convert raw speech audio into per-frame latent vectors. First, audio is transformed into mel-spectrograms. The DCA-VAE then encodes these into a structured latent space that disentangles speech content from speaker identity and prosody, producing rich audio embeddings for the DiT.
Waveform → Mel-Spectrogram → DCA-VAE → Latent Embeddings
🔊
Why DCA-VAE over a simple CNN?

A standard CNN encoder produces entangled features — content, identity, and prosody are mixed together. The DCA-VAE learns to disentangle these factors via contrastive learning, producing a latent space where speech content (what phoneme) is separated from speaker characteristics (voice timbre). This gives the DiT cleaner conditioning signals and better control over lip sync.

Per-frame alignment: The mel-spectrogram is windowed so that each video frame at 25fps gets its own audio context window. The DCA-VAE encodes each window into a latent vector za ∈ ℝd for every frame. These latent embeddings become the audio conditioning tokens for the Diffusion Transformer in Stage 1.
LANDMARK PRIORS
Landmark VAE + DCA-VAE ENCODING
Stage 1 needs three types of information: what is being said (DCA-VAE audio latents), who is speaking (reference landmarks encoded through the Landmark VAE), and head orientation (pose landmarks also encoded through the Landmark VAE). Both VAEs map their inputs into compact latent spaces where the DiT operates.

Identity-aware lip shapes: The Landmark VAE learns that different people form the same phoneme differently — thin lips vs. full lips, wide jaw vs. narrow jaw. By encoding reference landmarks into the same latent space where generation happens, the DiT can produce landmarks that match both the audio content AND the person's unique facial structure.
DIFFUSION TRANSFORMER
Generating Landmarks via Flow Matching STAGE 1
Instead of directly regressing landmarks, Stage 1 uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) trained with flow matching. The DiT operates in the Landmark VAE's latent space — it learns to denoise landmark latents conditioned on audio, reference, and pose. At inference, it flows from Gaussian noise to clean landmark latents, which the VAE decoder maps back to 131 (x, y) coordinates.
Diffusion Transformer architecture
Flow matching: noise → landmark latents
Why flow matching over direct regression? Direct regression (e.g., L1 loss) produces averaged, blurry landmark trajectories — the model hedges between multiple plausible motions. Flow matching learns the full distribution of possible landmark sequences, producing sharper, more natural motions. The DiT can capture the multimodal nature of speech-to-motion mapping (the same audio can produce slightly different but equally valid lip movements).
STAGE 1 TRAINING
Flow Matching + Landmark VAE TRAINING
Stage 1 training has two phases. Phase A: Train the Landmark VAE to encode/decode 131-point landmarks into a compact latent space (reconstruction + KL loss). Phase B: Freeze the VAE and train the DiT with flow matching — the model learns to predict the velocity field that flows from noise to ground-truth landmark latents, conditioned on audio.
Training formulation
Phase A — Landmark VAE
● Input: 131 × 2 landmark coordinates
● Encoder → latent z ∈ ℝd
● Decoder → reconstructed landmarks
● Loss: Lrecon + β · LKL
Phase B — DiT flow matching
● VAE frozen — DiT operates in latent space
● Sample t ∈ [0,1], interpolate zt = (1−t)·noise + t·z
● DiT predicts velocity vθ(zt, t, audio, ref, pose)
● Loss: ‖vθ − (z − noise)‖²
🎞️
Why a latent space?

Raw landmarks are 131 × 2 = 262 dimensions — high-dimensional and sparse. The VAE compresses them into a smooth, dense latent space where nearby points correspond to similar facial configurations. The DiT can then generate in this well-structured space, producing smoother and more coherent landmark sequences than direct regression in coordinate space.

ALIGNMENT MODULE
Warping References to Target Pose STAGE 2
The alignment module takes N reference images and warps each one to match the target landmark pose. It uses SPADE normalization to inject landmark structure, predicts dense motion fields, and applies weighted aggregation across all warped references.

N = 25
Why multiple references? A single reference image may not contain all the appearance information needed (e.g., the person's profile, or their mouth wide open). Using N references from different angles and expressions gives the alignment module more appearance data to draw from. The weighted aggregation learns which reference is most useful for each spatial region.
TRANSLATION MODULE
From Aligned Features to Pixels RENDERING
The translation module takes the aggregated warped features and refines them into the final face image. It uses AdaIN (Adaptive Instance Normalization) to inject audio features — encoded through the same DCA-VAE — at multiple scales, ensuring lip shapes match the speech. This is where audio directly influences pixel-level rendering.
Translation module architecture
🎨
AdaIN: painting with audio

AdaIN works like a style transfer mechanism. The DCA-VAE audio latents modulate the statistics (mean and variance) of intermediate feature maps. Where SPADE injects spatial structure from landmarks, AdaIN injects temporal speech information from the DCA-VAE's disentangled audio representation. Together, they ensure the mouth shape matches both the landmark pose AND the audio content.

Multi-source fusion: The translation module doesn't just refine a single warped image. It fuses information from all N reference images (via the alignment module's aggregated features), the target landmarks (via SPADE), and the audio (via AdaIN) — producing a face that combines the best appearance data from all sources.
RENDERING LOSSES
Five-Component Training Signal LOSSES
Stage 2 is trained with five complementary losses that balance realism, identity, and stability. Each loss targets a different aspect of image quality — from pixel accuracy to high-level perceptual similarity to adversarial realism.
Loss breakdown with weights
Balancing act: The weights of these five losses are carefully tuned. Too much GAN loss → artifacts and instability. Too much L1 → blurry outputs. Too much perceptual → color shifts. The paper's default weights represent a stable equilibrium found through ablation studies.
IDENTITY & COMPOSITING
Preserving Identity, Blending Seamlessly OUTPUT
The final output is composited back onto the original video frame using a Gaussian mask centered on the face region. This ensures seamless blending between the generated face and the original background/hair. Multiple reference images help preserve identity across varied poses.

Soft blending: The Gaussian mask has a smooth falloff — fully opaque at the face center, gradually transparent toward the edges. This avoids hard seams between the generated face and the original frame. The feather radius σ controls how gradual the transition is.
