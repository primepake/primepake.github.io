THE VISION
What Does AI Creator Do? OVERVIEW
AI Creator generates photorealistic 3D talking avatars driven by audio. Given a short training video of a person and a new audio clip, it produces a video where the person speaks with accurate lip sync, natural expressions, and realistic head motion. Unlike 2D methods that paste or warp face textures, AI Creator uses a NeRF-based 3D representation that understands the full geometry of the head.

Three motion signals, one avatar

A convincing talking avatar requires three independent motion signals working in harmony: lip movements (driven by audio phonemes), facial expressions (eyebrow raises, squints, smiles — controlled by blendshape coefficients), and head pose (nods, tilts, turns — optimized from 3D morphable models). AI Creator handles all three through dedicated encoding streams that feed into a unified NeRF renderer.

The core challenge: Talking avatar generation must simultaneously handle precise lip-audio synchronization, expressive facial animation, natural head movement, and 3D-consistent rendering — all while preserving the person's identity from the training video.

AUDIO ENCODER
Audio-Visual Encoder AUDIO
The audio encoder converts speech into per-frame lip-sync embeddings. It uses a pretrained lip-reading network as its backbone — a model originally trained to predict speech content from silent video. This gives it a deep understanding of the audio-visual correspondence between sounds and mouth shapes. A cosine similarity discriminator provides feedback to ensure the embeddings faithfully represent lip movements.

Why a lip-reading backbone?

A standard audio encoder learns speech features in isolation. But by starting from a lip-reading network pretrained on video, the encoder already understands the mapping between sounds and visual mouth shapes. The cosine similarity discriminator then fine-tunes this: it compares the audio embedding with ground-truth lip features and pushes them to align in a shared embedding space. When cosine similarity is high (close to 1.0), the audio embedding accurately predicts the lip shape.

BLENDSHAPES
Blendshape Expression Control EXPRESSION
Facial expressions beyond lip sync — eyebrow raises, squints, cheek puffs, nose wrinkles — are controlled by blendshape coefficients. These are a standard set of scalar weights that control deformation of different face regions. Crucially, blendshapes are decoupled from lip sync: the audio encoder handles the mouth, while blendshapes handle everything else.

Blendshape channel sliders — conceptual
Key insight: By separating blendshape expressions from audio-driven lip sync, the system can generate a person speaking with a smile, a frown, or raised eyebrows — independent of what they're saying. The lip region is controlled by audio; the rest of the face is controlled by blendshapes.
MASKED ATTENTION
Region-Specific Attention ATTENTION
To prevent audio features from leaking into the upper face (causing unnatural brow movements when speaking) and blendshape features from interfering with lip sync, AI Creator uses masked attention. The attention mechanism restricts which conditioning signals influence which face regions: audio → mouth region, blendshapes → upper face.

Why not let audio control the whole face?

Without masked attention, the audio signal can "bleed" into non-mouth regions — causing eyebrows to twitch with every syllable or cheeks to deform unnaturally. The mask acts as a routing table: audio features are only allowed to attend to mouth-region spatial positions, while blendshape features attend to the upper face. This gives independent, clean control over each region.

HEAD POSE
Head Pose Optimizer POSE
Realistic head motion is recovered through joint optimization of two complementary signals: 3DMM sparse landmarks (a 3D morphable model that fits key facial points) and dense optical flow (pixel-level motion between frames). The optimizer iteratively refines the head rotation and translation parameters until both signals agree.
Two-track optimization — convergence animation
25
Joint optimization: 3DMM landmarks provide coarse global pose (where is the head pointing?) while optical flow provides fine local motion (how are pixels shifting frame-to-frame?). By jointly optimizing against both, the system gets accurate, smooth head trajectories that neither signal could provide alone.
HASH-GRID NERF
Multi-Plane Hash Encoding NERF
Instead of a single 3D hash grid, AI Creator projects 3D points onto three orthogonal planes (XY, YZ, XZ) and performs hash-based feature lookups on each. Features from all three planes are concatenated and fed to an MLP that predicts color (RGB) and density (σ). Multi-resolution levels capture both coarse structure and fine detail.

Why multi-plane instead of full 3D?

A full 3D hash grid with N cells per side requires N³ entries — expensive in memory and slow to query. The tri-plane decomposition reduces this to 3×N² entries while still capturing 3D structure through the intersection of orthogonal features. Each plane captures a different "view" of the scene: XY sees the frontal face, YZ sees the side profile, XZ sees the top-down structure. Their combination reconstructs the full 3D representation.

VOLUME RENDERING
Ray Casting & Compositing RENDERING
To render an image from the NeRF, rays are cast from the camera through each pixel into the 3D scene. Along each ray, sample points query the hash-grid for color and density. These are composited using alpha compositing (front-to-back accumulation) — dense regions contribute more color, transparent regions let background through.
Camera → rays → sample points → pixel color
Single ray detail — alpha compositing
Volume rendering equation: For each ray, the final color C = Σᵢ Tᵢ · αᵢ · cᵢ, where Tᵢ is the accumulated transmittance (how much light passes through), αᵢ = 1 − exp(−σᵢ · δᵢ) is the opacity at sample i, cᵢ is the color, and δᵢ is the distance between samples. The conditioning features (audio, blendshape, pose) modulate the MLP that predicts cᵢ and σᵢ.
RESTORATION
Portrait Restoration Network RESTORE
Raw NeRF renders can lack fine details — pores, hair strands, subtle skin textures. The portrait restoration network enhances the NeRF output to recover these details. A soft Gaussian mask then blends the restored head region with the original torso and background, creating a seamless final frame.

Why blend, not replace? Simply pasting the restored head onto the background creates visible seams at the boundary. The Gaussian mask creates a smooth falloff: full strength at the face center, gradually fading to zero at the edges. This soft transition hides any resolution or color mismatches between the NeRF-rendered head and the original background.
RELIGHTING
NeRF Relighting LIGHTING
AI Creator goes beyond avatar animation — it can also relight the rendered face to match the lighting conditions of a reference image uploaded by the user. By decomposing the NeRF's radiance into intrinsic components (albedo, normals, shading), the system can re-render the avatar under novel illumination, making it blend naturally into any target scene.

How does relighting work with NeRF?

Traditional NeRF bakes lighting into the learned radiance — changing illumination means retraining. AI Creator instead trains the NeRF to decompose appearance into view-independent albedo (surface color), surface normals (geometry orientation), and shading (light interaction). At inference, a lighting estimation network extracts the illumination environment from the user's reference image (as spherical harmonics coefficients), and the shading component is re-computed under this new lighting. The result: same face, different lighting, no retraining.

User workflow: The user uploads a reference photo with the desired lighting (e.g., warm studio light, cool outdoor light, dramatic side lighting). The system estimates the lighting environment from this image and applies it to the avatar's NeRF rendering — the face appears as if it were physically present in that lighting condition.

Why so many losses?

Each loss prevents a specific failure mode: lip sync loss (BCE) ensures mouth shapes match audio phonemes; reconstruction loss (L1) prevents color drift and ensures pixel-level accuracy; perceptual loss (MSE + LPIPS) captures high-level structural similarity that L1 misses; pose loss (L2) keeps head motion smooth and accurate. Without any one of these, quality degrades in that specific dimension.

SUMMARY
End-to-End Recap SUMMARY
AI Creator transforms audio into a photorealistic 3D talking avatar through five stages: encode the conditioning signals, condition the NeRF with region-specific attention, render via volume rendering, restore with portrait enhancement, and relight to match any target scene.
Complete pipeline — encode → condition → render → restore → relight
Key insights: (1) Decoupling lip sync from expression via masked attention gives independent control. (2) Multi-plane hash encoding makes NeRF rendering efficient. (3) Joint pose optimization from landmarks + optical flow yields smooth head motion. (4) Portrait restoration + soft blending bridges the quality gap to photorealism. (5) Intrinsic decomposition enables relighting from any reference image without retraining.
